3.4 Detect & treat outliers (IQR method)¶

Age should logically be between 0-110, and BMI should be realistic (roughly 10-60). We cap values using the IQR rule plus hard logical bounds.

In [9]:
def iqr_bounds(series):
    Q1, Q3 = series.quantile(0.25), series.quantile(0.75)
    IQR = Q3 - Q1
    return Q1 - 1.5*IQR, Q3 + 1.5*IQR

for col in ['Age', 'BMI', 'BloodPressure_Systolic', 'Cholesterol', 'LengthOfStay_days']:
    lower, upper = iqr_bounds(df[col])
    print(f"{col}: valid range ~ [{lower:.1f}, {upper:.1f}]")
    df[col] = df[col].clip(lower=max(lower,0), upper=upper)

# hard logical bounds
df['Age'] = df['Age'].clip(0, 110)
df['BMI'] = df['BMI'].clip(10, 60)

6. Key Insights (Storytelling)¶

  1. BMI and Cholesterol show a positive relationship - patients with higher BMI tend to have higher cholesterol readings, a pattern consistent with established clinical understanding.
  2. Length of stay varies by department - some departments (e.g. Cardiology, Orthopedics) show noticeably longer average stays than others, likely reflecting the nature of treatments involved.
  3. Smokers show a higher readmission rate than non-smokers in this dataset, consistent with smoking being a known risk factor for complications.
  4. Age distribution is fairly broad, covering young adults to elderly patients, with no single age group dominating the dataset.
  5. Data quality issues were common in raw data - missing values, inconsistent text casing (e.g. 'male' vs 'Male'), duplicate rows, and impossible values (like age above 150) all had to be fixed before any analysis could be trusted. This step is often 60-80% of real-world data science work.
